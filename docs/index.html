Attacks on health care in Ukraine: pilot results

Shwetha Mangalesh · MPA 2479 Applied AI for Public Policy, Brown University · Audience: UN OCHA, Centre for Humanitarian Data · Data as of 28 Oct 2026

The pilot uses three things: ReliefWeb reports on Ukraine, which carry the text of situation reports, press releases and statements; the SHCC record of attacks on health care in Ukraine, coded by analysts at Insecurity Insight; and a test of screening 20 of the 2022 reports for attacks on health care, by hand and with a language model.

Policy question. How can the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) obtain complete and current data on attacks on health care to guide humanitarian response decisions?

Text source

ReliefWeb: 11,062 reports on Ukraine since 2022

ReliefWeb, run by OCHA, carries situation reports, press releases, analyses and statements from UN agencies, NGOs, governments and the media. Its API returns the text of each report, not coded incidents. Counts are of reports whose primary country is Ukraine, by the report's own date. The pilot uses the 4,122 reports from 2022.

The text is what the ReliefWeb page shows. For many situation reports this is a summary, and the full report is an attached PDF. Report text belongs to its original sources and is not published here.

Coded record

SHCC: 3,146 incidents in Ukraine

Insecurity Insight's Ukraine attacks on health care file on HDX (CC BY-SA), covering 25 Nov 2016 to 28 Sep 2026, downloaded 28 Oct 2026. Each row is an incident coded by analysts, with the date it happened and the date it was entered.

Of the 981 incidents in 2022, 580 were entered in 2022 and 401 later. Entries arrive in batches: 31 of the 2022 incidents were entered on 9 Apr 2022, and 20 on 2 Oct 2023, so part of the 2022 lag may reflect back-filling rather than coding delay.

Where the 2022 attacks on health care were recorded

SHCC incidents in 2022 by oblast. In 2022 the record holds 583 incidents in which a health facility was damaged or destroyed, 9 attacks on children's hospitals, 131 attacks on ambulances or emergency medical services, and 166 health workers killed or injured.

Coordinates in the public file are censored, so incidents are shown by oblast. 258 of the 981 incidents in 2022 (26%) give no oblast and are not on the map. Incidents recorded by Kyiv city district are shown in Kyiv city; SHCC records Crimea and Sevastopol together as the Crimean Peninsula. Colour classes are quintiles of each layer's non-zero values. Screening test

20 reports coded by hand and by a language model

Question coded. Does the report describe at least one specific attack on health care? (yes / no). The codebook combines the SHCC Attacks Data Codebook (2019) definitions and event criteria with eight rules for coding whole reports. Rule B2: a "yes" needs an identifiable event (a place, a date, or a particular facility, worker or vehicle); a total alone is "no".

The 20 reports were coded by hand first (4 yes, 16 no) and committed before any model output existed. The model, claude-opus-5-5 through the Anthropic API, then coded the same text with the same codebook, zero-shot. A keyword rule (a health term in the same sentence as a harm word) gives a simple dictionary comparison.

Where the model and the hand codes disagree

3 reports, 3 of them coded yes by hand and no by the model. In 3 the model's reason cites rule B2: the report refers to attacks on health care in general or as a total, with no single event.
